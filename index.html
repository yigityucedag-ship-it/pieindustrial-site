Case study
Honest failure

The proxy promised twelve winners. The solver failed all twelve.

A 1,063-generation run, driven by a fast in-loop proxy, sent its twelve best candidates to CalculiX certification. Real torsion safety factors came back 0.97–1.28, all below the 1.5 bar. Nothing shipped.

Then the system turned on its own proxy:

  • measured it 40.1% optimistic — 12 of 12 candidates over-promised
  • found its reported accuracy understated ~81× against reality
  • found its confidence band ~48σ too narrow
  • filed all 12 failures in the failure museum as evidence for the next run

Most engineering-AI pipelines would have published the mirage. PIE's architecture exists so it can't.

The honesty apparatus

Held to its own rules.

PREREGISTRATION

Envelope, load cases, safety bar, baseline and the exact claim ceiling — frozen before iteration 1, so the claim cannot be adapted to the result.

GROUND-TRUTH LEDGER

Append-only, content-addressed record of every real-solver certification — passes and failures. Proxy results cannot enter by construction.

CLAIM COMPILER

Every outbound number maps to a JSON path in a hashed artifact and is machine-checked: ALL_TRACED, or named failures. No manifest, no publication.

BLIND TWO-LANE REVIEW

An independent verification lane re-runs the rulers itself rather than accepting reported numbers. No result grades itself.
